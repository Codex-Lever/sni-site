CodexLever · Synthetic Neurodynamic Intelligence (SNI) · letters-up prototype

A mind that learns to read from letters, and knows what it doesn't understand

It starts with 26 letters and nothing else: no pretrained embeddings, no backpropagation, no LLM. It grows its own word meanings by reading, gets names from your yes/no answers, tells you which kind of not-understanding it has, and proposes connections for you to confirm. What it has read

Two books, in order. First a primer in a generated mini-language with 17 kinds of things, then three real public-domain children's books. Words are discovered as letter strings; nothing tells it what any word means.

Reading changes its senses (word meanings drift). “Re-encode” replays every confirmed answer through the new meanings, like sleep replay.

Connections it proposes

Its own hypotheses, from reading: words that read alike may be the same kind of thing, and the words between two concepts (“fish live in water”) can signal a relation once one example is confirmed. You only say yes or no.

Teach one fact yourself

The mind asks “'bird' lives-in what?”, hears your word as a proposal, and you confirm it. The words it read between them become cues for that relation.

Held-out exam

15 words per category that no teacher ever named, and that the primer never put next to their category word. Read-only. Plus strings it has never seen: made-up words, misspellings, letter noise.

Locality audit, run live

Every learned number (letter edges, word familiarity, word meanings, association edges, relation cues, concept engrams and edges) changes only through registered update functions that read their own pre/post activity, own value, own trace and one global modulator. The audit checks each, statically and with randomized probes.

Kinds of not-understanding

  • form: never read this string; is it word-like, or a variant of a word I know?
  • exposure: read it, too rarely to know what it is like
  • meaning: I know what it is like, not what it is; or two names compete
  • relation: I know what it is, not how it connects
  • surprise: a sentence or a judgement went against my prediction

How it works

Five layers of explicit memory, each grown by local plasticity, each inspectable.

1 · LettersLetter-context engrams predict the next letter. They give a sense of spelling: is a string word-like, or a variant of a known word?
2 · WordsA word engram is born the first time a letter string is read, with a fixed random identity code. Frequent words with unpredictable neighbours habituate into “glue”.
3 · MeaningEach word's context vector grows by Hebbian co-activation with its neighbours' codes: its own embedding, from reading alone.
4 · NamesThe SNI concept engine perceives a word through that vector and learns names and facts from ±1 only, asking when it is unsure.
5 · ConnectionsWords that read alike suggest the same kind. Patterns between words, once confirmed for one relation, suggest the next.
