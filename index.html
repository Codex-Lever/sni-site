Synthetic Neurodynamic Intelligence

A memory-centric alternative to training ever-larger networks: explicit engrams you can read and edit, local plasticity instead of backpropagation, learning from yes / no judgements, and a mind that knows what it doesn't know. Both demos run the real research engine, in Python, inside your browser.

SNI Live Lab

Teach a brain words with yes and no. It asks when it doesn't know, generalises from one example, answers in multiple hops with a readable trace, and lets you cut a single memory.

What SNI tests

Explicit memoryEvery memory is an engram or a typed edge: inspectable, lesionable, with provenance.
Local plasticityNo global loss. Each update reads only its own pre/post activity, value, trace and one modulator.
Learning from ±1Teachers judge propositions the system makes; they never write labels or facts into memory.
Typed ignoranceForm, exposure, meaning, relation and surprise are separate signals, each with its own question.
Δθ = F(upre, upost, θ, eθ, mt)

Honest status

These are research prototypes. Every benchmark on these pages is exploratory and says where SNI loses as well as where it wins. Classical baselines beat it on raw accuracy on our small benchmarks. Its case rests on behaviour under change: learning from yes/no, readable uncertainty, and edits whose effects stay local. That is the subject of the next pre-registered stage.
