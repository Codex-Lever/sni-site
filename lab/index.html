CodexLever · Synthetic Neurodynamic Intelligence (SNI) · research prototype v1

A brain that learns words from “yes” and “no”

No backpropagation, no pre-training, no LLM. Every memory is an explicit engram you can see, query and cut. It learns only from your ±1 judgements, with local plasticity rules, and it asks when it meets something it does not know. This is the real research engine (2,849 lines of Python) running in your browser through WebAssembly.

Its own answers, right vs wrong

    Autopilot = three scripted teachers who know the answers and only ever say ±1 (or a word when the brain asks). Same channel as you.

    Held-out exam

    Words the brain has never been shown, from every category it has a name for, plus words from categories it was never taught. Read-only: the exam changes nothing in memory.

    Surgery on a single memory

    Every answer is a readable path through explicit edges, so the effect of cutting one edge can be computed before you cut it, and only answers that use it change. Pick an edge (or click a learned word on the map).

    Offline consolidation

    Sleep replays memory offline: merges redundant engrams, downscales unconfirmed edges, prunes what fell below threshold. Edges that three different teachers confirmed are consolidated and protected. It also runs on its own every 50 judgements.

    Locality audit, run live in your browser

    Every change to a learned number goes through a registered update function. The audit checks each one, statically and with randomized probes, reads only its own pre/post activity, its own parameters, its own eligibility trace and the global modulator. No gradients, no global loss.

    What you can and cannot do to it

    The engine's public interface has no method that writes a label, a fact or a target into memory. You can show it a word, answer its question, and say +1 or −1. That's all.

    How it works

    Intelligence as the self-organisation of explicit memory under prediction error and local plasticity, not as gradient descent on static weights.

    1 · PerceiveA word arrives as a frozen vector (GloVe). Perception never learns; it is only the senses.
    2 · Match or askEngrams compete to explain the input. If none does, the novelty profile says “novel” and the brain asks you what it is.
    3 · ProposeYour word is not written into memory. It becomes a proposition the brain says back, which you must confirm with +1.
    4 · Local plasticityA ±1, scaled by surprise and teacher reliability, updates only the edges on the answer's path. New engrams and edges form only on a confirmed proposal.
    Δθ = F(upre, upost, θ, eθ, mt)

    The SNI Locality Rule: every learned quantity changes only from its own pre- and post-synaptic activity, its own value, its own eligibility trace and one global modulator mt (the teacher's ±1, surprise, reliability). Audited in CI and in the Inside tab.
